Rio de Janeiro

Christ the Redeemer (Corcovado)
Book the van/train up early — first departures around 7:20am beat both the queues and the midday cloud cover.
Directions →
Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açúcar)
Cable car in two stages from Urca. Good any time of day; sunset is the busiest slot.
Directions →
Copacabana Beach
Right by Miramar. Walk the beachfront promenade towards Copacabana Fort.
Directions →
Ipanema Beach
A short taxi/metro ride from Copacabana — calmer, well known for the sunset at Arpoador rock.
Directions →
Escadaria Selarón (Selarón Steps)
Colourful mosaic staircase in Lapa/Santa Teresa — worth combining with the Santa Teresa tram.
Directions →
Barra da Tijuca
Local tip from Mihir: larger and less busy than Copacabana/Ipanema — good if you want more space.
Directions →
Praia Vermelha
Small sheltered beach near Urca, right below Sugarloaf — calm water, good views of the cable car.
Directions →
Joatinga & Prainha
Mihir’s personal favourites — secluded, few tourists know about them. More of a trek to reach but worth it for a quieter beach day.
Directions →
Pedra da Gávea hike
Almost a full day hike with amazing views at the top — a real challenge, so be careful and go with a guide if unsure.
Directions →
Dois Irmãos hike
Take a favela motorbike up to the start of the trail, then a much shorter hike to the top — great views over Vidigal and the coast.
Directions →
Pedra do Telegrafo
Very short hike to the famous “hanging picture” photo spot — quick add-on if you’re nearby.
Directions →
Pedra do Sal
Free live samba/pagode street party — only happens on Mondays, so time it right if you want to go.
Directions →
Sunset at Arpoador
A must-do — the rocks between Ipanema and Copacabana are the classic spot to watch the sunset with a crowd cheering it on.
Directions →
Boteco choppy & açaí
Get a “choppy” (ice-cold draft beer) at any boteco, and try açaí — Mihir rates Oakberry but there are lots of good brands around town.
Directions →
Flamengo at the Maracanã
Catch a Flamengo game at the Maracanã if the fixtures line up — book through a hostel/hotel rather than turning up on the day.
Directions →
Glória market (Sundays)
A nice Sunday-morning market at Glória — good for a wander if you’re free.
Directions →
Paragliding — Tijuca Forest
Paraglide or hang glide from Tijuca National Forest, landing on São Conrado beach — highly recommended by Mihir.
Directions →
Safety tips (from Mihir, local friend)
Most of Zona Sul (Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon) is relatively safe if you’re street smart — avoid wearing jewellery. Ipanema/Leblon is wealthy and quite safe. Be more careful in Lapa, especially at night. Uber works everywhere; the metro is great and a fun experience — you can tap in contactless.

Foz do Iguaçu

Cataratas do Iguaçu (Brazilian side)
Book ahead at cataratasdoiguacu.com.br — ~R$131/adult (foreign visitors). Open Mon–Fri 9am–4pm, weekends 8:30am–4pm. Quietest 9–9:30am or 2–4pm.
Directions →
Macuco Safari (boat to the falls)
Inside the park — a boat ride right up to the base of the falls. Bring a dry bag; you will get soaked.
Directions →
Parque das Aves (Bird Park)
Walk-through aviaries right next to the falls entrance — good half-day add-on, toucans included.
Directions →
Itaipu Dam
One of the world's largest hydroelectric dams — panoramic tour is the quick option, further out from the falls.
Directions →
Remember: no ride-hail access inside the national park (see Itinerary tab) — arrange transfers with Sanma Hotel or an official taxi.

João Fernandes Beach

Geribá Beach
Lively surf beach with a good stretch of bars and restaurants right on the sand.

Tipping & money norms

  • Restaurants: 10% "serviço" is usually already added — check before tipping again.
  • No service charge shown? 10% is polite, not obligatory.
  • Taxis/rideshare: round up, no expected tip.
  • Hotel porters: R$5–10 per bag.
  • Small vendors/street food: carry small BRL notes.
  • Foz park entries & official taxis: card usually fine.
